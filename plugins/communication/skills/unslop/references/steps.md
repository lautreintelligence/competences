## Step 0 — Surface Tics

Three patterns to hunt:

### Pattern A: Punchline em dashes

Em dashes used to deliver a dramatic conclusion.

Diagnostic: Does the dash introduce the "punchline" of the sentence? If yes, it's a punchline em dash.

```
❌ "The project transformed the company—forever."
✅ "The project transformed the company. Nothing went back to the way it was."

✅ FINE (aside): "The project—launched in March despite objections—transformed the company."
```

Actions: Replace with period, semicolon, colon, or comma. Or rewrite the sentence entirely. Aside em dashes (interruptions, clarifications) are CORRECT — leave them.

### Pattern B: Unnecessary juxtaposition

The "it's not X, it's Y" formula where the negation adds nothing.

Diagnostic: Mentally delete the negative half. If the meaning holds, the negation was unnecessary.

```
❌ "It wasn't just about waiting—it was about remembering."
✅ "It was about remembering. Her voice, the way she used to read the headlines aloud."

❌ "It's not merely a tool, it's a revolution."
✅ "It's a revolution in how we think about the tool."
```

Action: Delete the negation. Keep ONLY the affirmation and develop it.

### Pattern C: Mechanical triads

Three items listed, the last one longer than the first two.

Diagnostic: Is the structure "short, short, long-and-dramatic"? If yes, it's a mechanical triad.

```
❌ "clarity, precision, and an unwavering commitment to excellence"
✅ "clarity and precision" (drop the third)
✅ "the kind of clarity you don't have to reread" (convert to concrete prose)

❌ "tranquility, serenity, and a profound sense of peace"
✅ "tranquility and serenity" (or better: replace both with something concrete — see Step 1)
```

Actions: Reduce to 2, expand to 4+, redistribute into prose, or remove the list entirely.

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "La réponse était simple — trop simple."
- ✅ "La réponse, d'une simplicité presque suspecte, me laissa perplexe."
- ❌ "Ce n'était pas un simple outil, c'était une révolution."
- ✅ "L'outil avait tout changé. On ne travaillait plus de la même façon."

### Worked Example (Step 0)

```
BEFORE: "The old man sat on the bench—a bench that had witnessed countless
sunsets. It wasn't just about waiting, it was about remembering. The park
offered tranquility, serenity, and a profound sense of peace."

AFTER:  "The old man sat on the bench, a bench that had witnessed countless
sunsets. He could almost remember her voice, the way she used to read the
headlines aloud. The park offered tranquility and serenity."

CHANGES:
• "bench—a bench" → "bench, a bench" [punchline em dash → comma]
• "It wasn't just about waiting, it was about remembering" → developed into
  concrete affirmation [unnecessary juxtaposition removed]
• "tranquility, serenity, and a profound sense of peace" → "tranquility
  and serenity" [triad broken]
```

## Step 1 — Abstraction Trap

AI has read everything but experienced nothing. It defaults to abstract, conceptual words because they require no sensory capability to deploy. The result is text you literally cannot visualize.

**Richard Price's rule**: "You don't write about the horrors of war. No. You write about a kid's burnt socks lying in the road."

**Quantified target**: Replace 25–50% of abstract nouns with concrete ones (things you could hold, smell, or draw). Every paragraph must contain at least one concrete image.

**The mental image test**: Read each sentence. Can you form a picture? If not, the sentence is probably abstract.

```
❌ "The park offered tranquility and serenity."
→ No image. What does "tranquility" look like? Nothing.

✅ "An empty fountain stood at the center of the park; pigeons picked at
bread crumbs near his feet."
→ You see the fountain, the pigeons, the crumbs.

❌ "the warmth of his memories kept him comfortable"
→ "Warmth of memories" is an abstraction pretending to be sensory.

✅ "he pulled his coat tighter"
→ A physical action that implies the same thing.
```

**Critical rule**: If removing the abstraction kills the sentence, the sentence was empty. Delete it or invent a concrete replacement that is faithful to the meaning.

**Exception**: Some abstractions carry irreplaceable meaning. Leave them. The target is 25–50%, not 100%.

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "L'innovation transforme notre quotidien."
- ✅ "Mon grand-père écrivait des lettres ; je dicte des messages à ma montre."

## Step 2 — Harmless Filter

Reinforcement learning has lobotomized AI's vocabulary of strong emotion and judgment. The adjectives are tepid, the opinions absent, the conflict avoided. The result reads like a sedative.

**Target**: Add AT LEAST ONE clear instance of conflict, cynicism, or weirdness. Words should ignite something in the reader's mind.

**What to look for:**
```
❌ "a rewarding and stimulating experience" (anesthetic)
✅ "an experience that cost him two nights of sleep and a friend" (you feel something)

❌ "remarkable results" (empty)
✅ "results that made the competitor nervous" (conflict)
```

**Red flag words**: "remarkable," "significant," "enriching," "stimulating," "innovative." They say nothing. Replace them with what they actually mean.

**Rules:**
- No insults or profanity. That's a cheap resource.
- Be politically incorrect IF the text calls for it. Not for provocation.
- Conflict is like salt: essential, but destructive in excess. One instance often suffices.

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "Le projet s'est déroulé dans de bonnes conditions."
- ✅ "Le projet a failli capoter trois fois. La quatrième, on a eu de la chance."

## Step 3 — Sensory Betrayal

AI strings together sensory claims that technically fit but fail to track how things actually feel. It describes by statistical association, not experience. Silk from a spiderweb isn't smooth — it's sticky and elastic. Cutting a tomato with a dull knife? Hilariously resistant.

**The decisive question**: For every sensory description, ask: "What would SURPRISE someone who only knows this thing from reading about it?"

```
❌ "a cool breeze" (generic, association-level)
✅ "a cool breeze that found the gap between his collar and his neck"
(specific, localized, you feel it)

❌ "he sat on the old bench" (flat)
✅ "The slats bowed slightly under him the way worn wood does when it's
been rained on too many times" (surprising: old wood flexes, it doesn't
just look old)

❌ "the soothing sound of the waves"
✅ "the crash of the waves — louder than you'd expect up close" (betrayal)
```

**Actions:**
- If the sensory description is a statistical cliché → replace with a surprising detail
- If you can't find an honest sensory betrayal → remove the description rather than inventing a fake one
- Unpleasant or ambiguous sensations are often truer than pleasant ones

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "L'air frais du matin caressait son visage."
- ✅ "L'air piquait les narines — ce froid humide de novembre qui sent la terre retournée."

## Step 4 — Forced Callbacks

AI doesn't trust the image to do the work. It personifies objects or attributes unearned emotions to force literary resonance. Two variants:

### Variant A — Emotion pasted on

```
❌ "The carpet had lines you felt bad stepping on."
→ Nobody feels that. The emotion isn't earned.
✅ "The carpet had clean lines, as if nobody ever walked on it."
→ The image is enough. The reader concludes on their own.
```

### Variant B — Object with memory

```
❌ "He picked up the pan, a pan that still remembered the last thing it burned."
→ Pans don't remember. The AI is forcing literariness.
✅ "He picked up the pan. A black crust still clung to the bottom."
→ Same information, no personification.
OR (humor option):
✅ "He picked up the pan — same burn mark as always, like a reproach."
→ The personification is acknowledged as a joke.
```

**Three options when facing a forced callback:**
1. Keep the object, remove the personification
2. Keep the personification, avoid the awkward tense shift
3. Make a joke of it

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "Elle ressentit une profonde tristesse en voyant la maison vide."
- ✅ "La maison vide. Même l'écho de ses pas semblait étranger."

## Step 5 — Subtext Vacuum

AI doesn't trust the reader. It shows a scene, then explains what the scene means. The reader gets robbed of the pleasure of understanding.

**The rule**: If the image does the work, the explanation is redundant.

```
❌ "He looked at the empty seat beside him, feeling a deep longing for
his late wife."
→ "feeling a deep longing" kills the subtext that "empty seat" created.

✅ "He looked at the empty seat beside him and rested his hand on the wood."
→ The hand on the wood IS the longing. The reader gets the reward.

❌ "She clenched her jaw, clearly irritated by the remark."
✅ "She clenched her jaw."

❌ "The office was empty, which gave an impression of solitude and
abandonment."
✅ "The office was empty. Not a pen on the desks."
→ Replace the explanation with a SECOND concrete detail that reinforces
the image.
```

**Actions:**
- When an explanation follows an image → delete the explanation
- When an internal state is named ("wanting," "feeling," "hoping") → replace with external behavior that implies the state
- "He desperately wanted to leave her" → "He glanced at the door and back at her."

**FR Examples:**
- ❌ "Elle détourna le regard. Elle ne voulait pas qu'il voie ses larmes."
- ✅ "Elle détourna le regard."

🔴 TRUST THE READER. Subtext is a gift, not a gap to fill.

## Complete Worked Example: Full Transformation (Steps 0→5)

This end-to-end example shows how all 6 steps combine on a single passage:

**ORIGINAL (AI-generated):**
```
The old man sat on the bench—a bench that had witnessed countless sunsets.
It wasn't just about waiting, it was about remembering. The park offered
tranquility, serenity, and a profound sense of peace. While the evening
brought a cool breeze, the warmth of his memories kept him comfortable.
He looked at the empty seat beside him, feeling a deep longing for his
late wife.
```

**FINAL (after all 6 steps):**
```
The old man sat on the bench, the same bench for forty years now. The
slats bowed slightly under him the way worn wood does when it's been
rained on too many times. He could almost remember her voice, the way
she used to read the headlines aloud, even the obituaries. An empty
fountain stood at the center of the park; pigeons picked at bread crumbs
near his feet, fighting over the stale bits. The evening brought a cool
breeze that found the gap between his collar and his neck, and he pulled
his coat tighter. A jogger passed without looking at him. He, in turn,
looked at the empty seat beside him and rested his hand on the wood.
```

This text passes AI detectors (Pangram, QuillBot: 100% human). More importantly, it reads like something a person wrote sitting in that park.
